# Cinematic color language

Use this as a naming and reasoning guide, not a mandatory catalog. Select only terms supported by the current reference.

## Naming hierarchy

Prefer a specific cinematic name over a bare hue:

- **Foundational and high-impact**: crimson, Tibetan blue, emerald or ancestral green, bright yellow, pure black, titanium white, royal purple, vivid orange, cherry gray pink, olive green, coral, amber, indigo, caramel, burgundy, cyan.
- **Muted and neutral**: gray blue, mouse-tail grass green, oat, taupe, rose gold, khaki, Morandi pink, linen, cream white, smoky red, gray purple, bean-paste red, light coffee, lead gray, mint, glacier blue.
- **Cinema and grading families**: teal-orange, red-green neon contrast, sepia vintage, high-contrast monochrome, silver-wing blue, wasteland yellow, ultra-cool minimal, instant-film faded, film-grain color, Japanese fresh blue, gothic dark, Monet painterly, vaporwave, golden-age warm, Nordic cold, acidic designer color, extreme low saturation, cel color, neon pink-blue, uncanny night-road color.
- **Climate and natural light**: golden hour, blue hour, Tyndall beam, aurora green, fiery-cloud red, morning-fog gray, deep-sea blue, forest deep green, starfield black, thunderstorm purple, coral rainbow, autumn orange, polar ice blue, firefly yellow, eclipse violet, iridescent refraction.
- **Material, fantasy, and speculative**: bioluminescent green, dream soft pink, alchemical gold, ghost white, meteorite black gray, glass gradient cyan, code green, abyss violet, crystal transparent, velvet dark red, neon circuit, spacetime-rift blue, holographic magenta-blue, liquid silver, rose glass, magic fluorescent violet.

Use Chinese terms naturally in the output. The English lighting-look name remains separate.

## Color structure

Describe color as a hierarchy rather than a filter:

- **Dominant color**: controls the largest tonal or spatial mass, usually shadows, environment, or background.
- **Supporting color**: separates planes, carries midtones, or balances the dominant hue.
- **Accent color**: limited to practicals, highlights, reflections, props, or focal edges. Keep it sparse enough to direct attention.
- **Skin/material anchor**: retains a believable neutral or warm reference unless intentional stylization is clearly visible.

Do not quote a rigid ratio when the image does not support measurement. If useful, describe the balance approximately as broad dominant mass, smaller support field, and sparse accent rather than forcing `60/30/10`.

## Cinematic contrast

Identify the mechanism that creates depth:

- warm subject against cool environment;
- cool subject against warm practicals;
- complementary teal-orange, red-green, blue-yellow, or violet-gold;
- analogous hues separated by luminance and saturation;
- low-saturation field punctuated by one controlled vivid accent;
- monochromatic hue separated through black level, density, material response, and specular highlights.

Avoid full-frame uniform tint. Preserve neutral anchors, readable blacks, highlight color separation, and skin or primary-material identity.

## Lighting response to color

Translate the palette into physically plausible lighting feedback:

1. Identify whether the color comes from a real emitter, environmental bounce, production design, atmosphere, or grading.
2. Let the key light establish form and skin/material readability. Its hue may remain neutral even when the grade is strongly colored.
3. Let ambient or fill carry environmental color without lifting blacks excessively.
4. Use rim or accent color only when a motivated source or visible reference edge supports it.
5. Describe colored reflections on wet ground, glass, metal, skin sheen, fabric, smoke, fog, dust, water, crystal, or other relevant surfaces.
6. Describe volumetric color only when haze, mist, rain, dust, underwater scatter, or visible beams are present.
7. Protect highlight channels from clipping and prevent saturated colored light from erasing texture.

Keep grading and lighting distinct: a blue shadow grade does not prove blue illumination; an orange highlight grade does not prove a tungsten key.
